# FIGMA MAKE DESIGN PROMPT — PREMIUM TATTOO STUDIO WEBSITE

Design a complete, polished, responsive website UI for a premium tattoo studio in the United States.

This website will primarily receive visitors from paid Facebook and Instagram advertising. Its primary business objective is to establish immediate credibility, showcase the quality of the tattoo work and artists, and convert visitors into consultation/appointment leads.

The primary conversion journey is:

**Advertisement → Website → Discover Artists/Work → Book Appointment → Submit Lead Form → Continue Conversation on WhatsApp**

The website must feel like a premium tattoo brand, creative studio, and interactive editorial experience—not a generic local-business website.

---

# 1. VISUAL REFERENCE AND CREATIVE DIRECTION

The design direction is inspired by premium editorial tattoo, fashion, photography, and creative-studio websites.

The desired experience is:

* premium
* editorial
* artistic
* cinematic
* minimal
* modern
* slightly underground
* confident
* photography-led
* highly interactive
* sophisticated

Do NOT create:

* a generic Bootstrap business website
* a colorful SaaS landing page
* a dashboard aesthetic
* excessive rounded cards
* glassmorphism
* neon effects
* bright gradients
* glowing borders
* excessive decorative icons
* gimmicky animations
* cluttered layouts

The interface itself should remain restrained so that tattoo photography, artist portraits, typography, spacing, and motion provide the personality.

---

# 2. STRICT DARK-MODE DESIGN SYSTEM

The entire website must be dark mode.

Use a charcoal-based palette rather than pure black everywhere.

Suggested direction:

Primary background:
approximately #111111 / #121212

Secondary surfaces:
approximately #171717

Raised surfaces where necessary:
approximately #1C1C1C

Primary text:
approximately #F5F5F2

Secondary text:
approximately #B7B7B2

Muted text:
approximately #858582

Borders:
subtle translucent white/grey.

The primary visual accent should essentially be **white/off-white**.

Do not introduce unnecessary brand colors.

Natural color contained within tattoo photography may remain visible and should provide much of the visual variation.

---

# 3. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography must be one of the strongest elements of the design.

Use:

1. A strong editorial/condensed display typeface for major headings.
2. A clean modern sans-serif for body copy, navigation, forms, and interface elements.

Use:

* enormous section titles
* uppercase display typography
* strong scale contrast
* compact labels
* deliberate letter spacing
* controlled line heights
* short confident copy

Major words such as:

ARTISTS

WORK

THE STUDIO

HOME CALL

BOOK

should become part of the visual composition rather than behaving like ordinary section headings.

Do not sacrifice mobile readability for styling.

---

# 4. RESPONSIVE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Design separate intentional compositions for:

### Mobile

Intimate, vertical, touch-first, highly photographic.

### Tablet

Editorial hybrid, touch-first, with selective two-column compositions.

### Desktop

Spacious, cinematic, composition-driven, with stronger layering, asymmetric layouts, and hover interactions.

### Ultrawide

Maintain structural content constraints while allowing selected photography and oversized typography to bleed beyond the normal content grid.

Do NOT simply shrink desktop for mobile or stretch mobile for desktop.

---

# 5. NAVIGATION

Create an extremely minimal navigation system.

Desktop navigation:

LOGO

Artists
Work
Studio
Contact

BOOK APPOINTMENT

BOOK APPOINTMENT should receive stronger visual emphasis without using a bright accent color.

Use either:

* white button with dark text
* or a refined high-contrast outlined treatment

Mobile navigation:

LOGO                                      BOOK   MENU

Keep it extremely clean.

Consider smart sticky behavior:

* visible initially
* slightly compact after scrolling
* can gently hide while scrolling downward
* returns when scrolling upward

Navigation must never interfere with important photography or typography.

---

# 6. HERO — HIGHLY INTERACTIVE

The hero must create an immediate premium impression.

Do NOT design a generic:

headline + paragraph + button + stock photo

hero.

Create an immersive, cinematic first viewport using strong tattoo/studio photography and editorial typography.

Possible composition:

large studio wordmark/name

CUSTOM TATTOOING
[LOCATION]

very short supporting statement

BOOK APPOINTMENT

secondary:

EXPLORE ARTISTS ↓

The hero should feel alive.

Design for subtle motion such as:

* photographic reveal on load
* typography reveal
* slight image scaling
* layered typography
* controlled parallax
* subtle scroll response
* staggered entrance of small interface elements

Animation must remain sophisticated and restrained.

No bouncing, spinning, glowing, or flashy effects.

---

# 7. ARTISTS MUST COME IMMEDIATELY AFTER HERO

Do NOT put About, Location, Services, testimonials, or Home Call immediately after the hero.

After the hero, transition directly into:

# ARTISTS

This is one of the most important parts of the website.

The studio will feature its **Top 10 tattoo artists**.

The artists should feel like the stars of the website.

Do NOT create ten identical cards in a conventional grid.

Each artist should receive substantial visual presence.

Artist information should include:

* portrait/profile photography
* artist name
* short biography
* tattoo specialties/styles
* VIEW GALLERY
* BOOK THIS ARTIST
* optional Instagram/social link

---

The design may use a combination of:

* large artist portraits

* alternating layouts
* controlled horizontal browsing where appropriate
* oversized artist names
* image transitions
* selective sticky behavior
* subtle image scaling

Example artist presentation:

BLACK & GREY

PORTRAITURE

Artist 02:
information left / image right.

Artist 03:

Continue variations while preserving a coherent design system.

On mobile, prioritize a vertical editorial experience with large portraits rather than tiny artist cards.

---

Create an Artist Detail / Gallery screen.

# 10. BOOK THIS ARTIST

Every artist should have:

BOOK THIS ARTIST

This is different from the site's general:

BOOK APPOINTMENT

When BOOK THIS ARTIST is selected, the booking experience should already know which artist the visitor requested.

The visitor should NOT have to select the artist again.

Design the booking screen to communicate:

REQUEST AN APPOINTMENT
WITH [ARTIST NAME]

Include a small artist portrait or visual reference.

---

# 11. GENERAL TATTOO PORTFOLIO / WORK SECTION

After artist discovery, introduce a more visually dense:

# WORK

section.

This should contrast with the spacious artist sections.

Artists:

spacious / personal / editorial

Work:

dense / immersive / exploratory

Use an asymmetric image composition or editorial masonry system.

Allow selected images to receive substantially more visual space.

Artwork should be tappable/clickable.

Where relevant, the image viewer can display:

ARTIST: [NAME]

VIEW ARTIST

BOOK ARTIST

This allows portfolio browsing to become another artist-discovery and booking route.

---

# 12. GENERAL BOOK APPOINTMENT CTAs

The website must support visitors who do not know which artist they want.

BOOK APPOINTMENT should therefore remain available naturally throughout the website.

Potential placements:

* navigation
* hero
* after artist exploration
* portfolio section
* Home Call section
* studio/location section
* final CTA

Do NOT aggressively repeat giant booking buttons every few pixels.

Keep conversion opportunities visible but premium and restrained.

Example mid-page message:

NOT SURE WHICH ARTIST?

Tell us what you have in mind and our team can help.

BOOK APPOINTMENT →

---

# 13. HOME CALL SERVICE — IMPORTANT SERVICE OFFER

The studio offers a **Home Call tattoo service**.

This is an important business differentiator and should receive a dedicated editorial section rather than being hidden inside a footer.

The service should communicate clearly that:

* clients can request tattoo services at their location
* the studio can travel to locations across the United States
* the Home Call service is offered free of charge, according to the studio's service terms/availability
* visitors can begin arranging the service through the booking/consultation process

Create a premium section with a major heading such as:

# THE STUDIO

# CAN COME TO YOU.

or:

# HOME CALL

Supporting direction:

**Prefer your session at your location?**

Our Home Call service allows eligible clients to arrange tattoo services at their preferred location. We can accommodate requests across the United States.

**Home Call service is offered at no additional service charge, subject to scheduling, artist availability, location suitability, and studio confirmation.**

CTA:

BOOK A HOME CALL →

Secondary:

ASK US ON WHATSAPP →

Do not make this section look like a promotional coupon or cheap "FREE!" advertisement.

The word FREE should not become a giant colorful badge.

Communicate the benefit confidently and professionally.

Use editorial photography that could represent a professional mobile tattoo setup, artist preparation, or an appropriate client environment.

The section should reinforce professionalism and safety rather than suggesting informal tattooing.

---

# 18. FORM UI

Avoid standard browser-looking controls.

Use:

* charcoal input surfaces
* thin grey borders
* strong labels
* generous mobile input height
* subtle focus states
* clear accessible error messages
* restrained validation
* loading state
* successful submission state

Inputs should generally use minimal radius.

Avoid giant rounded pill inputs.

Submit CTA:

CONTINUE TO WHATSAPP →

While processing:

SENDING REQUEST...

Prevent duplicate submissions.

---

# 19. SUCCESS STATE BEFORE WHATSAPP

Do NOT immediately throw the visitor into WhatsApp after form submission.

First show confirmation.

Example:

# REQUEST RECEIVED.

Reference:

TAT-0842

Your consultation request has been saved.

Continue with our booking team on WhatsApp to discuss your tattoo, artist availability, scheduling, pricing, placement, references, and any additional details.

CONTINUE TO WHATSAPP →

The interface may automatically redirect after a short delay, but the explicit button must remain.

---

# 20. WHATSAPP HANDOFF

WhatsApp should open with a concise prefilled message such as:

"Hi, I just submitted tattoo consultation TAT-0842 through the website."

Do not place sensitive booking information directly inside the WhatsApp URL.

The website/backend should retain the submitted lead independently.

---

# 21. FLOATING WHATSAPP BUTTON — IMPORTANT

Add a subtle floating WhatsApp communication button throughout the public website.

This is primarily important on mobile.

### MOBILE POSITION

Place it near the **bottom-left corner** of the viewport.

It must:

* remain easily reachable with the thumb
* avoid interfering with browser controls
* avoid the iPhone home indicator
* avoid covering important content
* avoid overlapping form controls
* avoid competing visually with primary Book Appointment CTAs

Use safe-area-aware positioning.

Conceptually:

bottom:
approximately `max(18–24px, env(safe-area-inset-bottom) + 12px)`

left:
approximately `max(16–20px, env(safe-area-inset-left) + 12px)`

Do not place it flush against the screen edge.

### VISUAL STYLE

Do NOT use a giant bright-green floating bubble that destroys the monochrome design.

Create a refined interpretation.

For example:

small circular or compact rounded control

charcoal/near-black surface

subtle white/grey border

white WhatsApp icon

very subtle shadow/separation from photography

On hover/touch/intent, it may reveal:

**CHAT ON WHATSAPP**

If retaining recognizable WhatsApp green is necessary for immediate recognition, use it extremely sparingly—for example only within the small WhatsApp glyph rather than turning the entire control bright green.

### BEHAVIOR

Tap:

open the studio's WhatsApp conversation.

The floating button represents **general communication**.

It should not replace the formal Book Appointment → lead form → WhatsApp flow.

This distinction is important:

Floating WhatsApp = quick questions/general communication.

Book Appointment = structured consultation lead capture.

The WhatsApp button should remain secondary to Book Appointment.

### CONTEXTUAL BEHAVIOR

When a full-screen menu, gallery viewer, modal, or booking overlay is active, intelligently hide or reposition the floating WhatsApp control so it never obstructs controls.

On the booking form itself, consider reducing its prominence because WhatsApp is already the next step.

# 25. MOBILE-FIRST REQUIREMENTS

Most paid-ad traffic is expected from Facebook and Instagram on smartphones.

The mobile experience must therefore be treated as a first-class design.

Pay particular attention to:

* hero image cropping
* enormous but controlled typography
* artist portraits
* gallery composition
* thumb reach
* booking buttons
* floating WhatsApp button
* forms
* full-screen menu
* gallery viewer
* vertical rhythm
* performance
* in-app Facebook/Instagram browsers
* Safari browser chrome

Avoid tiny touch targets.

Use at least approximately 44×44px interaction targets.

---

# 26. IPHONE SAFE AREAS

The website must properly accommodate:

* iPhone notch
* Dynamic Island
* status bar
* rounded corners
* Safari browser chrome
* bottom home indicator

Design all fixed/sticky elements around:

env(safe-area-inset-top)

env(safe-area-inset-right)

env(safe-area-inset-bottom)

env(safe-area-inset-left)

This applies especially to:

* navigation
* hero
* mobile menu
* floating WhatsApp button
* sticky booking controls
* image viewer
* modals
* bottom controls

Do not place important UI directly against device edges.

---

# 27. TABLET DESIGN

Create a deliberate tablet layout.

Tablet must not simply be enlarged mobile.

Use:

* selective two-column artist compositions
* larger image proportions
* 2–3 column galleries
* generous whitespace
* touch-first interaction
* responsive typography
* appropriate navigation transformation

Portrait tablets can remain closer to mobile editorial composition.

Landscape tablets can begin introducing desktop-style split layouts.

Do not require hover for any functionality.

---

# 28. DESKTOP DESIGN

Desktop should feel substantially more cinematic.

Use:

* 12-column underlying grid
* asymmetric composition
* large negative space
* large artist portraits
* alternating image/text layouts
* oversized typography
* controlled visual overlap
* wider galleries
* occasional sticky editorial sections
* refined hover interactions
* subtle cursor-aware effects only where useful

Do NOT simply stretch mobile components across the screen.

---

# 29. MOTION SYSTEM

Design motion around three ideas:

REVEAL
SHIFT
FOCUS

Examples:

* image clip reveals
* small opacity transitions
* slight vertical text entrances
* controlled image scaling
* gallery transitions
* menu transitions
* artist transitions
* subtle parallax
* button inversion
* page transitions

Avoid:

* bouncing
* spinning
* glowing
* particles
* excessive 3D
* dramatic scroll hijacking

Motion should feel premium and editorial.

Provide a reduced-motion state.

---

# 30. PERFORMANCE-AWARE DESIGN

This website will contain significant high-quality photography.

Design with realistic performance constraints.

Plan for:

* AVIF/WebP
* responsive image sizes
* lazy loading
* image CDN/storage
* art-directed hero imagery
* limited initial preload
* optimized gallery loading
* lightweight motion
* avoiding unnecessary autoplay video
* avoiding enormous first-load payloads

The site must remain fast on mobile connections because paid-ad visitors may leave quickly if the first viewport loads slowly.

---

# 31. ACCESSIBILITY

Dark minimalism must remain accessible.

Design for:

* WCAG-appropriate contrast
* visible focus states
* keyboard navigation
* semantic controls
* form labels
* accessible errors
* image alt-text strategy
* reduced motion
* large touch targets
* readable body typography

Do not use low contrast simply for aesthetic minimalism.

# FINAL CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

The experience should feel like:

**an interactive tattoo editorial that happens to contain an exceptionally well-designed conversion system.**

The visual journey should be:

**BRAND IMPACT → TOP 10 ARTISTS → ARTIST WORK → TATTOO CREDIBILITY → HOME CALL OPTION → PHYSICAL STUDIO → CONSULTATION → WHATSAPP**

The conversion system underneath should be:

**DISCOVER → TRUST → CHOOSE → BOOK → LEAD CAPTURED → WHATSAPP**

Prioritize, in this exact order:

**Premium visual presentation → Tattoo portfolio credibility → Top 10 artist discovery → Booking conversion → Studio/Home Call flexibility → WhatsApp communication → Excellent mobile experience.**

Keep the final design original. Use the reference only for its editorial philosophy, hierarchy, photographic confidence, minimal interaction language, and cinematic pacing—not its branding, proprietary artwork, wording, or exact compositions.
